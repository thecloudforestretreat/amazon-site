Cuyabeno Wildlife Reserve

Choose Cuyabeno for flooded forest, lagoon landscapes and a wildlife experience built around canoe travel—often with a wider range of price points than remote Napo lodges.

Quick answer

Who is Cuyabeno best for?

Cuyabeno is a strong first Amazon choice for travelers who enjoy small-boat exploration and want multiple lodge categories. The long overland and river approach is part of the trip, and the lodge location, guide structure and group size strongly affect the experience.

Why choose it

Seasonally flooded forest, rivers and lagoons give Cuyabeno a distinct visual identity. Much of the wildlife search happens from canoes, allowing quiet movement along forest edges and waterways. It suits travelers who value time outside over luxury amenities.

What to expect

Expect shared transfers, warm and humid conditions, simple-to-comfortable lodges, forest walks and repeated canoe outings. Water level affects routes and landscapes. The reserve is large, and experiences differ between lodge zones, guides and itinerary lengths.

How to plan

Compare the full transfer schedule, departure point, canoe time, room type, electricity, hot water, guide language and maximum excursion group. Ask which fees and drinks are excluded and whether boots, ponchos and refill water are supplied.

Frequently asked questions

Cuyabeno

Is Cuyabeno a budget destination?

It offers a broad price range, but the cheapest headline price is not the whole cost. Compare transfers, fees, group size and inclusions.

How many days are enough?

Four or five days usually creates a better balance between transfer time and rainforest activities than the shortest programs.

Will I see pink river dolphins?

They occur in the region, but sightings are never guaranteed. Water conditions, location, timing and chance all matter.
